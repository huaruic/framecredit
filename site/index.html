What is FrameCredit?

FrameCredit is a local video processing tool that burns visible attribution into a video before it is uploaded to X. You save your X handle once, drop in a finished video, and FrameCredit produces an Attributed Export: a normal MP4 whose frames carry a compact X · @handle Creator Marker.

If the video is downloaded and reposted, the copied file still shows who made it. The marker is burned into the rendered frames rather than stored as metadata, so stripping metadata does not remove it, and viewers can search for your handle even when the repost carries no link back to you.

How FrameCredit works

Three steps, all on your own machine.

  1. Save your X handle once

    FrameCredit remembers it and previews the exact X · @handle identity before anything is burned in, so a typo never ends up in a long export.

  2. Drop in a video

    FrameCredit burns a compact outlined X · @handle marker into every frame, alternating between the two top corners every 30 seconds so it never parks in one spot or drifts across the picture.

  3. Publish the Attributed Export

    Take the finished file from ~/Movies/FrameCredit and upload it to X as usual. It is an MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio that keeps the source dimensions and audio track, with duration within 150 milliseconds of the source.

The Creator Marker

  • Reads X · @yourhandle, so viewers know both the platform and the searchable account.
  • Transparent outlined text. No opaque card sits behind it.
  • Alternates between top-left and top-right every 30 seconds.
  • Stays in the top corners, clear of the subtitle region.
  • Compact by design: it identifies you without obscuring the content.

Install FrameCredit

Requires Python 3.11+ with ffmpeg and ffprobe on PATH.

Install from source

FrameCredit is not yet published on PyPI, so install it from source with pip:

git clone https://framecredit.example/framecredit.git
cd framecredit
python3 -m pip install -e .

Create an Attributed Export from the command line

Point framecredit process at a finished video with your handle:

framecredit process input.mp4 --x-handle "@yourhandle" --output attributed.mp4

Or run the local drag-and-drop app

One command opens an English browser interface where you save your handle, preview the marker, and drag in a video:

framecredit app

The interface listens only on 127.0.0.1 and writes Attributed Exports to ~/Movies/FrameCredit by default. Keep the terminal running while you use it; press Ctrl+C to stop.

Processed locally, start to finish

Your video never leaves your machine. FrameCredit does all of its processing on your own computer: the local app binds to 127.0.0.1 only, the video is streamed to the FrameCredit process on the same computer, and nothing is sent to a remote service.

There are no accounts, no cloud processing, and no platform APIs. Unpublished footage stays unpublished until you upload the Attributed Export yourself.

What FrameCredit does not promise

FrameCredit's promise is Attribution Survival, not theft prevention. It makes your identity travel with an Ordinary Repost: a copied upload that keeps most of the content, including re-encodes and light edits. It does not try to make copying impossible.

A burned-in marker is pixels, not a hyperlink. It cannot be a clickable link or a QR code; a mention becomes clickable only in X post text, which an independently re-uploaded file cannot preserve.

Determined editing can defeat any visible marker. Someone willing to crop, cover, or reconstruct frames can remove attribution, and that remains outside what FrameCredit claims to solve.

Frequently asked questions

How do I keep credit when my video is reposted on X?

Burn your identity into the frames before you publish. FrameCredit adds a compact X · @handle Creator Marker to every frame, so an ordinary repost, even one that is re-encoded or lightly edited, still shows viewers who made the video and which account to search for.

Does FrameCredit upload my video?

No. FrameCredit runs entirely on your computer. The local app listens only on 127.0.0.1, your video is streamed to the FrameCredit process on the same machine, and the finished Attributed Export is written to ~/Movies/FrameCredit by default. Nothing is sent to a remote service.

What does the Creator Marker look like?

A compact single line of outlined text that reads X · @yourhandle. It uses transparent outlined lettering instead of an opaque card, stays readable over light and dark footage, and alternates between the two top corners every 30 seconds, away from the subtitle region.

Can a reposter remove the marker?

A determined editor can crop, cover, or paint over any visible marker, and FrameCredit does not claim otherwise. It changes the common case instead: ordinary reposts that download and re-upload your file, including re-encodes and light edits, keep your handle visible inside the frames.

What formats does FrameCredit output?

One MP4 file with H.264 video and AAC audio, chosen for broad upload compatibility. The Attributed Export keeps the source dimensions and audio track, and its duration stays within 150 milliseconds of the source video.

What do I need to run FrameCredit?

Python 3.11 or later, plus ffmpeg and ffprobe on your PATH. FrameCredit is not yet published on PyPI, so you install it from source with pip. It ships as a command line tool with an optional local drag-and-drop app started by framecredit app.

Where are Attributed Exports saved?

The command writes wherever you point --output. The local app writes Attributed Exports to ~/Movies/FrameCredit by default and shows the exact output path in its result panel, where you can open the finished video directly.
